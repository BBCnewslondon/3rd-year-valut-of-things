
Gravitational waves were first predicted in 1916 by Albert Einstein. They can be described as oscillations in the fabric of spacetime, These waves propagate at the speed of light.[1]![[Pasted image 20251030140900.png]]*Figure 1: The evolution of the different multipole terms of the gravitational potential for a simulated binary system. While the monopole (mass) and dipole (momentum) terms are constant or zero in the center-of-mass frame, the quadrupole term oscillates. This time-varying quadrupole moment is what sources the emission of gravitational waves, carrying energy away from the system. (Image credit: Armaan Sachdeva)* 

Gravitational waves are formed in a similar manner to electromagnetic waves. However the key difference is that Mass can only be positive. This means Mass is subject to conservation laws of momentum. As a result the gravitational monopole and dipole cannot emit any sort of radiation. A pulsating star (monopole) will not produce any Gravitational waves as the total mass is constant. An oscillating object (dipole) will not produce any gravitational waves as the center of mass cannot accelerate with some external force acting on it. With all this in mind the lowest order and dominant form of Gravitational wave emission would be quadrupolar. The strain $h$ would be proportional to the 2nd time derivative $\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}$ of the systems quadrupole moment$(I_{i,j})$ . Where $h\sim\frac{G}{c^4 r} \ddot{I}_{ij}$ . The $\frac{1}{c^4}$ term in the relationship really shows how weak gravity is. This is the reason why enormous astronomical objects are needed which have asymmetrically  accelerating mass distributions like a binary star system which is spiraling inwards or objects like black holes or neutron stars. For these systems the quadrupole moment will vary periodically as the masses orbit (Figure 1) , radiating the orbital energy away as gravitational waves and leading to the system to eventually death spiral and eventually merge.

The detection of gravitational wave strain  predicted to be $h\sim 10^{-21}$ for events such as the events previously stated. In order to measure this we are essentially measuring a displacement which is less than 0.001% the width of a proton. Detectors like LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA use power recycled , Fabry-Perot Michelson interferometers[2]. These work by splitting laser light and sending it down particular arms, where it is then made to resonate within Fabry-Perot cavities, which just increases the light's travel time before it recombines by a factor of 10. This causes the phase shift induced by any passing Gravitational wave to be amplified. The wave will strain the arms ever so slightly, breaking the tuned destructive interference and sending the signal to a photodetector.
